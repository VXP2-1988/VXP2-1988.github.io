Finished The Ritual by Adam Nevill yesterday. Spoilers for the book/movie, and CW for the typical horror stuff. Overall it's a solid 4/5.

#Plot Summary:
Four former college friends in their mid 30's start a hiking trip together in Sweden for their reunion. Three of them have stayed fairly close and have careers, partners, and families, but the main character Luke lives alone in London and has had a string of failed relationships and jobs. The differences between them, the unpreparedness and irreverence of his friends, and Luke's own selfishness and self-loathing cause immediate tensions. On the second day, one of the others twists his knee and they decide to cut through a woodland preserve to get home faster. Once inside they become completely lost and have terrifying nightmares of a tall black goat-like creature hunting them. They find strange things everywhere like a fresh elk ripped open and impaled on a tree and an ancient burned cabin with a taxidermy goat/human hybrid that they wake up kneeling in front of when they try to sleep there for the night. The monster picks them off one by one until only Luke and his friend Dom remain, taunting them by hanging the bodies of their friends from the trees. Luke manages to hurt the creature but it cracks his skull and takes Dom, leaving Luke alone. He is found by two young men and a woman who believe the creature is Odin and plan to sacrifice Luke to it with the help of the only remaining member of the creature's cult, an old woman in the woods. They torture him when he tries to escape and try to offer him on a cross to the creature, but the old woman gets disgusted with their disrespect and antics so she sets Luke free in exchange for getting rid of them. The wannabe cultists try to stop him but he steals their rifle, kills 2, and drives off the 3rd, then shoots the old woman when she attempts to summon the monster to deal with him. He escapes in a truck while the monster hounds him, and when it crashes into him and gets its head stuck he manages to injure its throat badly. He escapes from the forest and makes his way back to civilization.

#Review:
It was pretty great, though it was different from what I expected having watched the movie first. While the movie was more explicitly supernatural and the main character's journey was about facing his fear, the book had a more animalistic monster and the main character's journey was about finding meaning in his life and coping with his anger and the sense of social exclusion he feels. It wasn't a groundbreaking book, but it was very satisfying and competently written horror. The author did a good job not overusing violence and knew how to rely on suspense and describe situations or people inventively. It was also kind of nice to have a monster that hovered between the natural and the supernatural; it was ancient, intelligent, incredibly fast, and able to influence dreams but it was also "endangered" and confined to a shrinking forest, and ultimately it only had one old woman who still worshiped it. 

Luke's character growth was also good, although sometimes the internal monologue was overdone. In the start of the books he has a lot of bottled up rage, he hates himself and his job, and is jealous of his friends who don't let him forget that he hasn't done much with his life. He wants to get away from himself during the hike but can't. First there are verbal altercations, then he beats Dom after Dom goads him and that fight is mirrored in both a later altercation with the monster and the later beating Luke receives from the new cult. Each event contextualizes his own anger and helps him put the good and evil he's experiencing into perspective.

Luke is terrified of the new cult in the beginning, but over the course of his interactions with their leader he loses all respect for their goals and becomes openly derisive of their inconsistent philosophy. The animalistic brutality of the monster, the casual psychopathy of the new cult, and the ancient sadism of the old cult go from being something otherworldly to something very human and predictable.

Dom and Luke forgave each other before being separated, and that forgiveness combined with Dom's death and Luke's new understanding of himself becomes his basis for rejecting the monster, its new and old cults, and avenging his friends. He learns to appreciate his life and goes into the finale with a sense of purpose and fury which made for a fun ending.
