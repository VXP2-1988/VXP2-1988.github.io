To be hated is so close to being loved. They're almost indistinguishable. A polarity lying impossibly far from its opposite and yet so close they're almost the same. Like sides of a playing card. 

The old testament God was a terrible and awesome force, in the original meanings of the word. He inspired terror and awe. It's hard to explain the feeling of power, but you know it when you see it. It's the type of thing that you feel in your guts, standing on a mountaintop in a thunderstorm, watching the lighting split the sky open. You can almost see something inside those cracks of light in the darkness. It's not just electrons, charges, or plasma. There's Something living in the light and thunder. 

The Old God killed humanity like cattle. Wiped us out like disease-ridden animals. He turned people to salt, burned cities to the ground, turned the sky black and rivers to blood. He turned Satan on people to test them, called to people in the night, banished women and children to wander the desert, and drove kings so mad that they wandered like animals in the field. That god was an all-consuming fire in the heart. A sick fever infecting every thought.

When I was a child, I was taught to love that god. Taught to fear him. Taught that love and fear were the same. And when that fear turned to hate, the love still remained. Mingling together in a cognitive dissonance that I was also taught to embrace. Taught to embrace the contradiction of the Trinity. The one that is three but also one. Taught to embrace a god that wasn't even in one piece. Taught the sins of sleeping with the same sex, but also that He should enter your body and soul.

*Now I'm afraid that death didn't want me either.*

If you have enough time and thought, it's easy to lose faith. The contradictions of the bible become too much to stomach. The rituals seem empty. You watch as pedophile-priests are put on trial. Funds misappropriated and the righteous left to die. The bitterness fills your mouth when you put the sacrament wine to your lips. Kneeling before an old man with the cross used to torture the messiah hanging behind him. 

*Long ago, there was a priest named Eli in the service of the Old God. Eli had many evil sons who didn't know God, and mocked him. They disrespected his offerings, sinned against Israel, and slept with the temple women. God is only merciful sometimes, and it was the will of the lord to mark them for death. The sons would die by man.*

*Now a boy named Samuel was born to a righteous woman. In his youth he began to serve in the temple. He had God's favor, unlike Eli's treacherous sons. One evening, Eli was lying down in his own room. His eyesight was failing and he couldn't see. Samuel heard a voice calling him, and ran to help Eli, who hadn't called him and sent him back to sleep. This happened three times, and on the third Eli told Samuel that the voice was God's, and that he should lie there and listen. Samuel lay there, and in the darkness and silence the Old God told Samuel what he would do to Eli's household.*

And finally I died. But death never came for me. The story didn't end where it was supposed to. Where it should have. Where it needed to end.

I'm still here. I can't feel anything except for an icy, excruciating sense of pins and needles. All around me is pitch black and ringing silence. The only tastes and smells are copper and vomit. I have a sense of movement, like I'm being tossed on the caps of some slow ocean waves. Other times I can feel the vibrations of things bumping against me in the dark. I can't move and touch them, or fend them off. I can't scream.

I can feel them in my mind. It's all I really have left. Everything else is just an agonizing afterimage. Nerve damage imprinted on the brain. They're cold, slipping between the folds of my mind. They speak in a chittering language that would make me vomit if I still had a stomach. They are the cold dread right before you wake from a nightmare. They are the feeling of loss when you lose something you love. And they're utterly indifferent. I'm just a piece of psychic driftwood in a vast, howling ocean of emptiness. Filled with everything that was forgotten by God and slipped through the cracks. 

This, for all eternity.
