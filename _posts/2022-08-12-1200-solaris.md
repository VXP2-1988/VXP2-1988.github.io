"The time of cruel wonders was not yet over."

Just finished Solaris by Stanislaw Lem. In it, 4 scientists are being tortured by ghosts from their pasts in isolation on a space station while studying a planet-spanning intelligent ocean. The ending fizzled and veered into some metaphysics that went over my head, but otherwise it was an interesting read.

The whole book was stuffed with abject despair about the human condition, and our inability to comprehend anything that isn't like us. In this book, the interplanetary travel and awesome technological achievements of future humanity are worthless because humanity never changes. The main character sums up 100s of years of human galactic conquest with a quietly despairing "It wasn't worth it." No matter what humans do, they can't escape from themselves. And when they're confronted with the physical embodiment of their own minds, hell breaks loose.

The scientists are trapped because they're human. They're pretty terrible people, but their "sins" aren't the issue. The problem is that they're light years away from earth trying to contact an alien intelligence but they don't even understand their own thoughts. No matter how much they learn about science, they're still learning it with monkey brains.

Ultimately it was a dry, pessimistic, but interesting read. Some good quotes below.

"It must be torture for you, or actually even worse. For a torture implement is lifeless and innocent as a rock that can fall and kill you. But an implement that can have good intentions, that could love - this I couldn't imagine."

"Even the most supremely abstract, sublimely theoretical, mathematized achievements of science have in reality moved only a step or two away from a prehistoric, coarsely sensory based anthropomorphic understanding of the world around us."

"Do you ever happen to know if there was a faith in a defective god? A god whose deficiencies don't arise from his simple-minded human creators, but constitute his most essential, eminent character. This would be a god limited in his omniscience and omnipotence, one who can make mistakes in foreseeing the future of his works, who can find himself horrified by the course of events he has set in motion. This is a... cripple god. Who always desires more than he's able to have, and doesn't always realize this to begin with. Who has built clocks but not the time that they measure, who has built systems and mechanisms that serve particular purposes - but they too have outgrown these purposes and betrayed them. And has created an infinity that from being the measure of the power he was supposed to have - turned into the measure of his boundless failure."
