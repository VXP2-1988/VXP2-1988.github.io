*Hell is the fire light at the end of the tunnel, heaven is the darkness. We die and are drawn to it. Our souls are eaten like moths by a cosmic spider.*

Pastor Matt was telling another sermon about the light of God. The church was full this early in the morning, the morning sunlight dim from the single skylight high overhead. I struggled to stay awake while a message that I'd heard a hundred times in different forms emanated from the pulpit.

"Then God said, 'Let there be light, and there was light."

I picked up one of the pencils from the holder in the back of the pew and idly spun it. First in my right hand, then tried my left. The non-dominant hand was clumsier and the pencil dropped to the maroon carpet with a muffled clatter. The sound barely registered among the background noises of people shifting in the hard wooden pews, young children coloring, and the occasional cough. The pastor kept speaking.

"I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness, but will have the light of life."

I watched Matt speaking for a while. He was a younger man with eyes that seemed to shine, a replacement for our last pastor after some kind of church drama that I didn't care enough to follow. He'd probably just pissed off someone higher up who invented a reason to pry him out of his cozy little congregation. 

The light fixture next to the lectern was very bright, with a shade made of frosted glass. I could see a large number of insect and moth corpses collected in the base of the glass. A disgusting amount, how long had it been since it had been cleaned?

Matt's tone of voice was building in volume, an indication that he was about to finish. Thank God.

"The people dwelling in darkness have seen a great light, and for those dwelling in the region and shadow of death, on them a light has dawned.

Do not be afraid of the light, embrace it, cherish it, and feel its life-giving warmth! God's love radiates from it, and we only have to walk forward into it to feel his divine embrace!"

He paused, and smiled at all of us,

"My brothers and sisters in Christ, don't be afraid of going into the light!"

A few polite chuckles bubbled up at the awkward attempt at humor, and it was finally over. A row over, an enthusiastic "Amen!" came from Mr Davis and his wife. The benediction was given and we all filed out into the foyer.

I saw Mr Davis standing alone and went over to speak to him, and get some coffee from the ancient machine in the corner. The Davis's were both long-standing and well-respected members of the congregation. He was a brilliant man who had worked at some government agency as a programmer a long time ago, and now kept birds and tutored mathematics. He'd taught me trigonometry and calculus throughout high school, and I hadn't seen him since I'd moved away.

His eyes seemed brighter than I remembered, and as we talked I developed a slight headache behind my eyes. I poured myself another cup of coffee and asked how he'd been. He talked about his recent health problems.

The doctors weren't sure exactly what had precipitated it, but he had simply fallen at home and hit his head. His wife found him and called 911, and while in the hospital there were rapid complications that left him with multi-organ failure. He was clinically dead for a few minutes before he was revived. Then he died a second time and was brought back. They were concerned that he'd suffered brain damage, but he bounced back rapidly and made an almost full recovery.

I smiled and expressed my congratulations at his recovery, and he nodded. Then I stopped smiling. He told me that he had gone to heaven and seen God those few minutes he was dead. He said that he had seen a bright light appear above him and was drawn into it. While was was in heaven he could feel the warmth of God around him, and even feel his holy touch pulling him closer. How darkness surrounded him then and he was back on earth. And how he still remembers that light and longs to return to it.

I nodded politely in confusion. This was very uncharacteristic of him. He'd always been on the agnostic side. Both he and his wife came here more for the community, not the theology. I'd never speak it out loud, but maybe he'd sustained brain damage after all. My headache was becoming distracting, and the coffee wasn't helping.

His wife evidently finished some business in the office and came over to join us. She was equally brilliant but applied herself to the church office, taking care of bookkeeping, organization, and the church funds. 

She chimed in when she heard him talking about his hospital stay. She spoke about how his health problems had been a blessing in disguise. He was a different man, changed for the better. Whatever had happened to him while he was on death's door had turned him into a different person, and his enthusiasm and fervor for life had lit up all those around him. The light of God's love had illuminated both their lives and shown them the path of righteousness.

I nodded politely again and made congratulatory noises. I hardly recognized either of them. Maybe his change could be explained by his health problems, but the way that the reserved woman I remembered was now gushing about the light of God was strange. I was happy that they got some positivity out of the experience, but I was beginning to realize that I wasn't just having a headache. This was turning into a migraine. I hadn't had one since middle school but I remembered the pain, nausea, and flashes of blinding light very well.

I excused myself. Mrs Davis offered me an Advil from her purse, which I accepted. Mr Davis told me he'd be in touch and that he had some textbooks that he didn't need anymore if I wanted to come by and pick them up. Barely paying attention, I agreed and walked out to the parking lot. 

At home I took another Advil and closed all the curtains, then laid down. Even dim light made my eyes ache, flashes of lightning spider-webbed across my vision with every pulse of pain, and my stomach felt like it was being turned inside out. I puked into a trash can, and the force of my stomach contracting made my vision flash and spin.

I laid there for what felt like eternity. My phone rang, and the noise and light from the little screen were such an assault on my haywire senses that I threw the damn thing out of the room. Finally I fell asleep next to the trash can, the pain fading into a dull ache.

In my dream I was finally in blessed darkness. It folded around me and soothed my head. I lay in a comfortable space outside of reality and time, trying not to feel anything at all. My brain felt fragile, like any sudden movement would cause it to fall into pieces. I needed the rest, time to recuperate.

I gradually began to hear a sound like footsteps in the darkness. They came closer, and I turned towards the noise, but the darkness was absolute. I tried to speak but no words came out. But I could hear faint words in the darkness around me, that voice is familiar?

"Don't go... don't go... don't go... the light isn't..."

Then the pastor's voice spoke in my mind,

"Then God said, 'Let there be light, and there was light."

And the dream disappeared in a blinding flash that dragged me into wakefulness. It was late afternoon, and somehow the blinds had fallen off one of the windows. Maybe I'd been too violent trying to close it when I got home and dislodged it. The sun was positioned to shine directly into my room, although now it didn't cause me such pain. I looked at the bottle of ginger ale and bin full of puke, and spit to clear my mouth. Then went to wash my face.

In the hallway, I found my phone. My throw had broken the screen, and a bright spider-web of cracks had spread across it. Luckily it still worked. A missed call from Davis was there. A text from him listed out the textbooks he had for me to pick up. There were a few biology and ecology books I had no idea what do do with, but the calculus ones were recent enough for resale. I still felt a little sick, but decided that it was worthwhile. And maybe getting out of the house would keep me from focusing on the feeling. I called to setup a time with him.

I arrived at their house that evening. I had thought that they went to bed on the early side, but the house was ablaze with light. Even the yard was full of new light fixtures that looked recently installed. The house glowed from within with every color of bulb. Moths, mayflies, and other bugs swarmed around the fixtures and windows in the dark, and their tiny chitinous bodies littered the ground around the lights.

I picked my way down the long driveway, feeling uncomfortable. As I got closer to the house, I noticed the smell of something rotting. Maybe a squirrel or something had died in the bushes. I knocked on the door, looking back toward my car. The light around me was so glaring that it was difficult to see it parked on the unlit road.

The door opened and Davis stood in the entryway, but I wasn't paying any attention to him. When the door had opened, a thick wave of the rotting stench had rolled out over me. My eyes stung, stomach lurched, and I gagged. Through tears I looked past him in curiosity and disgust. The bodies of the birds he used to keep were littering the floor in various states of decay. A few huddled on high perches, looking down. 

I was struggling with concern, pity, disgust, fear, and confusion. As I stood there in the entryway, some of the insects around the outdoor lights found their way inside, buzzing around and casting tiny flickering shadows that were instantly snuffed out. I realized that Mr Davis was saying something,

"So sorry that our home is such a mess, we haven't had much time to clean lately I'm afraid. It's been so busy up at the church since Pastor Matthew came.

If you're in a hurry, I can just bring the books to you."

He turned towards the interior, his bare foot coming down on a tiny rotting ribcage with a wet crunch. Back in the kitchen, I could hear a radio sermon playing,

"The eye is the lamp of the body. So, if your eye is healthy, your whole body will be full of light, but if your eye is bad, your whole body will be full of darkness. If then the light in you is darkness, how great is the darkness!"

The remains of the migraine from earlier stirred behind my eyes, set off by the glaring illumination searing into my skull from all angles. This was a mistake. I didn't need these books and the smell wasn't helping either. This charnel house occupied the same location that I spent so many afternoons at as a teenager, but it was unrecognizable in this new light. Davis yelled something that I couldn't make out and then returned with a few books, his feet slick with black blood and feathers.

"There's one more stack, you can take these and I'll help you bring the rest out."

Without really knowing what I was doing, I took the books started walking back to the car, propelled by nausea and my returning migraine.

Davis came up behind me, carrying a few more textbooks. The sweet smell of rot wasn't as bad back in the yard but it still filled the air, drowning all else out.

"It's such beautiful weather we're having. I try to appreciate all I can nowadays. Life is such a precious gift. You know, my trip to the other side made me see so many things I didn't see before. It's easy not to notice these things, but even the air smells good on nights like these! Reminds me of heaven."

My stomach churned. With the beat of my heart flashes of lightning spider-webbed across my vision, accompanied by stabbing sensation in my eye sockets.

We arrived at the car and stacked all the books in the trunk. I didn't care anymore, I just wanted to get away but didn't want to offend someone I'd once respected by taking off prematurely. Maybe I'd contact somebody tomorrow to check on him or ask his wife if they needed help. I said goodbye and quickly drove off, not even bothering to set my GPS. Even the screen light was like a flash bulb going off in my head, and I knew the route home.

The drive was normally a nice one down a mountain road, but in the dark with a migraine and my crap headlights I had to concentrate to keep from driving off the road. Luckily there weren't any other cars out. A tunnel loomed ahead, a long old one with only enough clearance for passenger cars. Built too long ago to even have electric lights, and they wouldn't spend the tax dollars to fit them out here. In the dark away from the Davis' perpetual daylight I was feeling better, and turned on the radio just so that I could listen to it fade as I went deeper into the tunnel. A random pop song was playing, and it faded into distortion and then nothing but static as I passed through the mouth of the tunnel. Among the clicks of the tuner button and the static, a pastor's voice suddenly spoke,

"And you, LORD, keep my lamp burning; my God turns my darkness into light."

The signal shouldn't have been able to reach this far in. Surely I should be out of the tunnel by now? My sides and back prickled as I realized that I couldn't see anything ahead or behind me. The high beam lever shifted as I snapped it into the on position and the road immediately in front of the car brightened, but somehow nothing else changed. The car felt like it was going faster in the darkness, a rattling sensation was growing and the wheel vibrated under my hands. The illuminated dial of the speedometer swam when I looked at it, like I was drunk and had lost my balance. I stomped on the pedal hard and screeched to a halt, the anti-lock brakes chiming angrily at me for a second.

The radio chimed in uselessly, repetitively, "My God turns my darkness into light, my God turns my darkness into light, my God turns my darkness into light."

The voice had started off in the hearty, confident tones of a radio preacher, but the voice was sounding increasingly desperate, terrified. The word "light" was pronounced with such loathing and terror that I could feel it in my core. Was I repeating along after the voice?

I set the hazards and got out. Years ago when this road had frozen I'd run up it. Going through the darkness on foot had been unnerving then, but it was petrifying now. I took a step forward, trying to look for the exit, but I couldn't see any thing except my car - a small flashing pool in the otherwise indistinguishable blackness. The hazards blinked - on, off, on, off, on, off, off... off. Just like that, it was gone too. Only the radio voice remained. Stepping back towards the sound, there was only road and empty space. Then the voice, too stopped.

And underneath, I could hear the sound of footsteps that had been masked by the radio, hazard clicks, and engine noise.

I spun around with my heart pounding, trying to look for them, and in the opposite direction saw a pinprick of light. I sprinted towards it, feeling like it was dragging me forward with a hook in my stomach. I ran, feeling the same way that I had in the car, that I was going faster than I should be able to. 

The footsteps got faster, and the sound grew into the disordered pounding of a crowd's feet. It was gaining on me, and I could feel fingers swatting at my back, trying to grab me. Trying to pull me back. The light rapidly grew ahead. I was flying towards it at tremendous speed, spurred on by terror. I couldn't see what was beyond there due to the brightness and I didn't care. Didn't even pay any attention to the headache that had returned and was sending spikes through my eyes the closer I got to the light. I broke past the tunnel mouth, into the blinding light, and heard a collective scream behind me, "NO!". The anguish and pain in the voices almost made me stop, "Don't go into the light!"

I looked back, still running into the glare, and saw a mass of faces in the mouth of the tunnel that disappeared into the darkness. And every one of the faces were looking behind me in terror and disgust, the hideous light reflected in their eyes. My legs sank up to the thighs in something soft and warm, and I fell to my hands and knees with a wet sucking splash.

I looked towards the light, and all my fear of the things in the tunnel vanished.

I had fallen into a vast, roiling ocean of gore that extended in every direction to a far horizon. Above the horizon was a sickly colorless orb brighter and larger than any sun. It burned with a fire that made my skin burn and feel stiff, and my eyes sting. The migraine that had been worsening the closer I got to the light turned into a long keening cry of pain that felt like my brain itself was crying out. Reflections glinted off something in the distance around the light.

Around the orb was a massive web of threads that shone like razor wire, arranged in dizzying geometric patterns. The strands didn't occupy space, they *were* space itself, and so sharp that they seemed to cut into the eye. The web spun slowly around the shining orb, the perimeter dipping down into the sea from a distance of what looked like thousands of miles. As it tore through the sea, bodies from every species and time were dragged into into it.

But the faces in the tunnel were looking at something else entirely. The strands of the web shivered as a titanic thing dragged itself along the razor wire. It was like nothing on earth, a thing with too many legs made of meat and mercury-metal, wound tighter than a spring about to recoil. It shone in the sickly light orb, slick with blood and nauseatingly bright silver. It occupied the massive web, but I couldn't tell how large it was. It seemed simultaneously as large as the sun and so tiny that my skin crawled. 

As I stared in horror, it approached one of the balls of gore stuck in the web and a mouthpart extended out, sucking the ball in with a crunching, tearing, slopping sound that seemed to reverberate off the empty sky.

I realized that I was moving towards the light even while I stared in horror at the thing. I couldn't push myself away, couldn't move back into the safety of the tunnel's darkness. I looked down and saw tiny silver strands thinner then a hair running through the bloody ocean. They had hooked into me and were pulling me closer. Closer to the thing in its web. 

I thrashed at them but the more I moved the more they cut into me, getting tighter around my body. They bit until I was barely human anymore, just lacerated chunks of somehow still-conscious meat held together by silver. I stared up at the light in terror, and the headache reached a crescendo. My right eye popped with a sound I could feel through the bone in my skull, but even in the black blindness I could still see the light and the thing in its web around it. The entire world was a single solid note of noise that went on forever.

I sat up and vomited over the edge of the hospital bed. A nurse pursed her lips, finished her notes, put down her clipboard, and left the room. I passed out again.

I came to my senses up mid-conversation with Davis. He was wearing a visitor badge and had brought a card. We were talking about the light one the other side. He was smiling and I could feel tears on my face. I turned away from him and put the pillow over my head.

I was sitting in a consulting room talking to a doctor. I'd had an epileptic seizure while driving home that night and crashed. I'd sustained a head injury, and in the process of checking it out they'd discovered a brain tumor. My stomach formed into a hard pit as he told me I had months left to live. That I should put my affairs in order, maybe attend counseling. Prepare myself. I closed my eyes, and saw a spider web of divine light spinning around an alien sun on the back of my right eyelid, a thing perched in it waiting for me.

I didn't have long until I went into the light.
