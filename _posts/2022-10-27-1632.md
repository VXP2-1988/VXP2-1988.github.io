My dreams are always confused. The narratives shift and repeat. They split into parallel and perpendicular stories, I jump into different bodies, and there’s always something watching me. I haven’t been sleeping well for the past few years. It feels like my head is filling up with fragments of the things that I see when I close my eyes. I constantly question whether I’m awake or dreaming. It’s not that the dreams are so particularly vivid or lifelike that they can pass for reality, it’s more that my waking state of mind often seems similar to my dreaming one. I feel fragmented. The fabric of causality, the logical progression of cause and effect, isn’t continuous.

What does it mean to be real? If a thing can affect the world, does that make it real? Are gods real because people feel their presence, build temples, and kill in their names? Were infrared and ultraviolet not real until we could devise ways of detecting them without our eyes? What’s more real: a tangible sensation of something intangible, or something that can be observed with instruments. We can measure the bandwidth of a radio wave but not a prayer. 

How do I determine reality. Continuity, reproducibility, depth of detail, or whatever feels sanest? There are auditory, visual, tactile, and other types of hallucinations. Although dreams don’t matter because whatever your mind creates, your mind believes. No matter how illogical. But I can look for mistakes and inconsistencies in structure. So, that’s why I’m seated next to the corpse, my hand on its stomach feeling it cool. I took a sliver of light, opened its right arm. Sipped the dark blood and nibbled on the edges of the incisions. I watched through its eyes as I cut a piece of tongue and chewed thoughtfully. The black thing living in the blood made a sound like a siren, and then I was lying in bed with the alarm clock blaring that same noise. 

I blinked and my wife was lying in the bed across from me. I watched myself through her eyes. I could feel the hatred inside her mind, and she spoke:

“You’re right to be afraid. You’re alone and lost, and when you dream you’re more alone than at any other point in your life. Nobody follows you into a dream. But that doesn’t mean that nobody follows you out.”

The scene repeated again with sirens in the background. Then I was alone in the bed again. I could hear my wife leaving the house. I jumped up and ran outside in bare feet to stop her, pleading for her not to leave. But the driveway was empty. Her car wasn’t anywhere on the road. In fact, the road was completely empty except for a dark stain the size of a person. The sun was high, and I remembered that she leaves for work before dawn. And that I sleep alone.

Finally, I woke up. The alarm clock had been blaring for over an hour. For all the bizarre and upsetting dreams that leave me with a tangled pit in my stomach, I hate waking up more. There’s no relief in wakefulness, the sense of confusion and impending doom sits in my mind and body like a bad diagnosis. I laid there trying to sew my brain back together until the alarm became too much and forced me up and out of bed.

It takes a minute to register the image in the bathroom mirror. I look the same as when I went to bed, but that body isn’t mine. The thing that watches me in the dreams has turned from a demon outside, to a demon inside me. That body is mine but I don’t recognize it anymore. Like an autoimmune disease the mind rejects the body. I watch the imposter mimic me in the mirror while I brush my teeth, wash my face, and shave. It duplicates my movements, exaggerating them to mock me. I hate its teasing eyes and want to tear them out, but it’s safe behind the cold glass - like a hated criminal in the visitation area of a prison. The sting of the aftershave brings focus for a moment, and the imposter’s world and mine collide at the speed of light, becoming a single reality. The reflection walks out to get breakfast, and I sheepishly follow. 

I planned to call my wife and confront her about the things she told me this morning, but the timeline doesn’t match up. She would have left long before she spoke to me and I sleep in the other room, so it was likely another dream. Plus she’s not that cruel, it must have been someone else saying those things. I feel something watching me from the corner of the room I can never see, and I leave it alone. Reflections have to live somewhere when there isn’t a mirror around. But bills have to be paid and existential dread doesn’t keep food in the fridge. Someday I’ll look in the right corner, and then it won’t be able to run.

Work is a blur. I can’t remember who is who. Their dream selves and their other selves mix into a blend of hate and rage that I’m too exhausted to parse. I’m angry with one coworker because they told me I’m repulsive and need to shower more, but I think that’s a memory from another dream. I snapped at another who told me that I’m a waste of meat and better off dead. That doesn’t sound like her though, how many of these people have I spoken with? My manager walks by and talks with me, my reflection trapped in his glasses and trying to force its way into his eyes, into his brain. I don’t know how to warn him. He notices me staring,

“Is everything alright? Are you feeling okay?”
“Just tired, sorry.”

The reflection breaks out of the glass. It slips into the cornea, then through the retina. I feel like jumping up, screaming, pleading, forcing his eyes closed but it’s already done. He continues talking about a weekly report he needs. I watch dumbly as the parasite settles into his brain, changing the cast of his features and tone of voice. My own eyes stare out of his face now, and my eyes are dead. He won’t see God ever again now, and a piece of my soul is trapped on earth.

“I think both times would be good to add to the report. I’d prefer Excel rather than doing it on the web app.”
“Okay, I’ll get it done by Friday.”

He leaves. I feel like crying. My reflection stares at me from my PC screen, my phone, and every window I pass. It smiles when I smile, frowns when I frown, and when I look at it I can see it studying my weaknesses. I get some coffee from the break room, and wonder if I’m dreaming again. Nothing feels real, and I can’t remember why I was so upset a minute ago. I brew some coffee in the machine.

How do you test dreams? You pinch yourself, is the classic answer. Can’t wake up, you pinch yourself harder. My arm has welts on it now and I’m still in a room with my reflection watching me from a cleaned whiteboard. It can’t be real, I must be dreaming. It twists into disgusting shapes as I glare at it. I remember the sting of the aftershave this morning and look at the cheap coffee kettle. I hold down the safety switch that would turn it off once the water boiled and press my hand to the kettle.

How does a sensation turn into pain, and how can you trust it? Warm becomes hot and then scalding. Pain isn’t all the same, maybe this isn’t the right kind of pain. If a pinch can wake you from a normal dream, maybe something bigger can wake you up from reality. I can see the demon in the shiny whiteboard looks pained now, good. Maybe I won’t wake up but I can hurt it. Someone comes in, and I quickly remove my hand from the kettle and finish making coffee. It’s too hot to drink, so I take it back to my desk. 

The pain in my hand keeps the creature weak. It glares at me from my monitor whenever the screen goes dark but I can finish my work for the day.

I stop for gas on the way home. The total on the pump goes up as I watch it dispense. My workday plays in reverse, each minute and hour of my life tallied up and sucked into the machine to pay for fuel. The total reaches a maximum and stops, leaving me with a few hours of my life to drag home for myself. Why the hell do I drag myself to an office full of people who hate me just so that I can pay for gas to drive to the office. The machine watches me, 00.00 a row of spider-like eyes waiting for me to start. Why is it 00.00 if I just fueled up?

A young woman walks up with barely disguised frustration and asks if I’m going to take much longer. I blink and try to focus. The gas cap is on, my car is still running, and the total is $00.00 - I never started pumping. I apologized but she gives me a look that indicates she’s vaguely considering calling the cops and walks back to her car. I watch my reflection follow her back.

It’s dark when I’m getting home. I’m exhausted and my reflection’s eyes watch me from the passenger seat. I don’t know what to do tonight, I don’t want to sleep. What if more things follow me out of the dream? I pull up and bring the groceries in. I must be getting weak if groceries feel this heavy. My wife isn’t home and I don’t know where she is. And even if she was here I can’t remember which person is real anymore, the ones in the dream or the one that isn’t. I’d rather just be left alone. 

The house is dark and it feels like another nightmare. Despite the lack of light, I can still see my reflection in the windows. Something feels wrong. But when does it ever feel right. Existential dread doesn’t keep the fridge filled so I have to keep working. I sat next to the corpse and put my hand on its stomach, feeling it cool. I took my knife and opened the right arm, sipped the dark blood and nibbled the edges of the incision. If you can find a little detail in the dream that’s off, sometimes you can wake up, but you have to dig deep. I cut off a piece of tongue and chewed it thoughtfully. I’ve never had tongue before so it’s fascinating that my mind can simulate the taste.

I heard the wail of the alarm clock, and breathed a sigh of relief. This was going on longer than usual. I closed my eyes, and then opened them to the same scene. The sirens got louder, patterns of red and blue light throwing shadows in the dark house. The dream shifted violently in a way that made me feel sick, and I was the reflection, watching myself eat. It woke up, and I ceased to exist.
