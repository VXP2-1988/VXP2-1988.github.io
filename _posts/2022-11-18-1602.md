## Introduction
Arthur Shopenhauer was a German philosopher born in 1788. He is notable for his philosophy of pessimism. He was among the first thinkers to bring eastern philosophical ideas into a western work.

His father was a wealthy merchant who drowned in a canal in 1805 after going through a period of depression. Suicide was suspected. Shopenhauer was 16 or 17 years old. His relationship with his mother was terrible. He said his happiest years were when he was away from her. 

After his father's death, Shopenhauer threw himself into studying. He attended lectures on Medicine, Philosophy, Mathematics, History, Physics, and Astronomy, among others. He also knew German, English, French, Italian, Spanish, Latin, and ancient Greek.

In 1813 (age 25) he went to the country to work on his thesis, *On the Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason*. In it he stipulates the principle that **nothing is without a reason for what it is, it conditions how we perceive the "world of representation"**. This is a recurrent theme in his work.

Shopenhauer grew increasingly disillusioned with academic philosophy, and thought that "real" philosophers acquire problems existentially by reflection on their own life and experience, not through abstract concept. His work is distinguished by lived experience. 

During this time, Shopenhauer was introduced to Hinduism and Buddhism. He was particularly fond of the Upanishads, a portion of the Hindu scriptures.

His magnum opus *The World as Will and Representation* was published in 1818 (age 30). Much of his later work was based on this work he created in his 20s. His work is best understood as a single continuous piece. The book is separated into four parts: epistemology, metaphysics, aesthetics, and ethics.

These works were largely ignored and contributed to his pessimism, along with his mother's rejection. He attempted to distribute his ideas at the same time of his contemporary Hegel, whose ideas he disliked, but his lectures were largely empty. He left the University of Berlin in frustration.

In 1851, he published *Parerga and Paralipomena*, Greek for *Appendices and Omissions*. This was his first successful book, helped by his disciples excellent reviews. Despite the popularity he lived alone. He died in 1860 at the age of 72.
