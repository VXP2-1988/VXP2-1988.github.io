Alright, I have the blog set up to a satisfactory level now. I can do all the editing I want to do without being distracted by things that are off-center or the wrong color. Now to decide what to actually fill this blog with.

This was the end of school list that I made two weeks ago:
- Node vs Vue vs Angular
- personal blog
- IDS systems
- developer videos
- cybersec videos
- test servers
- interview questions
- login systems
- encryption algorithms
- what do you want to do

It was a list of my thoughts at the time. Software engineering was a useful class that introduced a lot of new concepts. Some of the more interesting ones were login systems, cloud services, and asynchronous code. Actually, that's a good starting off point. Let's go over the interesting concepts from your courses.

- Software Engineering 3155
    - Login systems, bcrypt.
    - Cloud services, Amazon s3, relational databases.
    - Database migration, github actions, CI/CD
    - Async javascript.
- Security 3200
    - Encryption algorithms.
    - NIST standards.
    - IDS systems.
- OS & Networks
    - Ports, sockets, writing client/server in C.

That's plenty of stuff to go over. I had planned to create this blog in Vue with a backend in Flask and an SQL database but Jekyll was faster to get running, and the importance of being able to write was greater than having an fully featured blog. This will do just fine for now. However, the ISS site may be a good candidate for Vue. Flask templates would be okay with 5-8 users but templates are a little outdated at this point. Plus, I learned a lot implementing the last Vue thing I worked with.

THM has a Snort module, that's a good place to start with IDS stuff. They recommend knowing network fundamentals and I'm confident in my understanding of packet, frames, TCP/OSI model, and subnetting. Aiming for one THM room a week is a good initial goal, after two weeks I can reevaluate and scale up or down. Setting up a Raspberry Pi could be good IDS practice, though they don't have a lot of processing power so I'd need to see if there's a workaround.

I'll need to meet with Steve to make a plan for the ISS website. Also need to look into QuickBooks XML integration. Don't have a lot of time for that, it's not a high priority but automating invoice creation would reduce my work and his in the long run, especially now that ServiceNow integation isn't something I'll need to imminently plan for.

Just take it week by week. There's a lot to learn but the decision to be excited rather than intimidated will help you out. Work on planning out the ISS website, doing THM rooms, and working at the tutoring center. That's more than enough to keep you busy for now. Remember to go back to your 2214 data structures for reference.
